High-Acyl vs. Low-Acyl Gellan Gum

High-acyl (HA) and low-acyl (LA) gellan gum are two commonly used functional types of Gellan Gum (E418).

Although both are forms of Gellan Gum, they can provide different gel textures and functional characteristics. The appropriate type depends on the requirements of the formulation.

High-Acyl Gellan Gum

High-acyl gellan gum generally produces softer and more elastic gels. It is commonly used when a smooth mouthfeel, soft texture, or suspension function is required.

  • Plant-based milk
  • Oat, almond, soy, and coconut beverages
  • Protein-containing beverages
  • Dairy alternatives
  • Suspension systems

Low-Acyl Gellan Gum

Low-acyl gellan gum generally produces firmer and more brittle gels. It is commonly selected when stronger gel structure or a clearer appearance is required.

  • Clear beverages
  • Fruit preparations
  • Firm gels
  • Structured food systems
  • Plant tissue culture

Key Differences

Characteristic High-Acyl (HA) Low-Acyl (LA)
Gel texture Soft and elastic Firm and brittle
Typical appearance More opaque Generally clearer
Main function Suspension and soft texture Gel structure and clarity
Plant-based beverages Common Less common
Clear beverage systems Less common Common
Plant tissue culture Less common Common

Choosing Between HA and LA

The choice between HA and LA should be based on the desired functionality rather than simply the product name.

For a plant-based beverage requiring suspension and a softer mouthfeel, HA gellan gum may be a suitable starting point.

For a clear beverage or a firmer gel system, LA gellan gum may be more appropriate.

Actual performance depends on concentration, salts, pH, processing conditions, and the other ingredients used in the formulation.
