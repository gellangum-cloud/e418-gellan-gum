Gellan Gum (E418): Properties and Functional Characteristics

Gellan Gum (E418) is a microbial polysaccharide widely used as a gelling, stabilizing, suspending, and texturizing agent.

It is commonly available in two functional types: high-acyl (HA) and low-acyl (LA) gellan gum. The two types can provide different gel textures and functional performance.

High-Acyl Gellan Gum

High-acyl gellan gum generally produces softer and more elastic gels. It is commonly considered for plant-based milk, dairy alternatives, protein-containing beverages, and suspension systems.

Low-Acyl Gellan Gum

Low-acyl gellan gum generally produces firmer and more brittle gels. It is commonly used in clear beverages, fruit preparations, structured food systems, and plant tissue culture.

HA vs. LA

Property High-Acyl (HA) Low-Acyl (LA)
Typical gel texture Soft and elastic Firm and brittle
Typical appearance More opaque Generally clearer
Common use Plant-based beverages Clear beverages and firm gels
Plant tissue culture Less common Common

Gellan Gum Dosage

There is no single dosage that applies to every formulation. The required level depends on the desired viscosity, gel strength, suspension stability, texture, processing conditions, and other ingredients in the formulation.
