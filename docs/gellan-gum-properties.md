# Gellan Gum (E418): Properties and Functional Characteristics

Gellan Gum (E418) is a microbial polysaccharide widely used as a gelling, stabilizing, suspending, and texturizing agent.

It is commonly available in two functional types: high-acyl (HA) and low-acyl (LA) gellan gum. The two types can produce significantly different gel textures and are therefore selected according to the requirements of the formulation.

## High-Acyl Gellan Gum

High-acyl gellan gum generally forms soft, elastic, and cohesive gels.

It is commonly considered for:

- Plant-based milk and dairy alternatives
- Protein-containing beverages
- Suspended beverage systems
- Soft gels and structured food systems

In beverage applications, HA gellan gum can help maintain the uniform distribution of particles and improve physical stability.

## Low-Acyl Gellan Gum

Low-acyl gellan gum generally forms firmer and more brittle gels than high-acyl gellan gum.

It is commonly used in:

- Clear beverages
- Fruit preparations
- Firm gels
- Structured food products
- Plant tissue culture

LA gellan gum is particularly useful when a clear appearance and stronger gel structure are required.

## HA vs. LA

| Property | High-Acyl (HA) | Low-Acyl (LA) |
|---|---|---|
| Typical gel texture | Soft and elastic | Firm and brittle |
| Appearance | More opaque or cloudy | Generally clearer |
| Common use | Plant-based beverages | Clear beverages and firm gels |
| Suspension | Very useful | Useful depending on formulation |
| Plant tissue culture | Less common | Widely used |

The actual performance of gellan gum depends on concentration, formulation composition, processing conditions, pH, salts, and other ingredients.

## Gellan Gum Dosage

There is no single dosage that applies to every formulation.

The required level depends on the desired viscosity, gel strength, suspension stability, texture, and processing conditions. Beverage formulations often use relatively low concentrations, while gel systems may require higher levels.

Small-scale trials are recommended when developing a new formulation.
