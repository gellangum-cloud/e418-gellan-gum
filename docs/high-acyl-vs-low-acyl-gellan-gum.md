# High-Acyl vs. Low-Acyl Gellan Gum

High-acyl (HA) and low-acyl (LA) gellan gum are two commonly used functional types of Gellan Gum (E418).

Although both are derived from the same microbial polysaccharide, they can provide very different gel textures and functional performance.

## High-Acyl Gellan Gum

High-acyl gellan gum generally produces softer and more elastic gels.

It is commonly used in applications where a smooth mouthfeel, soft texture, or suspension function is important.

Typical applications include:

- Plant-based milk
- Oat, almond, soy, and coconut beverages
- Protein-containing beverages
- Dairy alternatives
- Suspension systems

HA gellan gum is particularly useful when the formulation needs to keep insoluble particles or nutrients evenly distributed.

## Low-Acyl Gellan Gum

Low-acyl gellan gum generally produces firmer and more brittle gels.

It is commonly selected when a stronger gel structure, clear appearance, or defined gel texture is required.

Typical applications include:

- Clear beverages
- Fruit preparations
- Firm gels
- Structured food systems
- Plant tissue culture

## Key Differences

| Characteristic | High-Acyl (HA) | Low-Acyl (LA) |
|---|---|---|
| Gel texture | Soft and elastic | Firm and brittle |
| Typical appearance | More opaque | More transparent |
| Main function | Suspension and soft texture | Gel structure and clarity |
| Plant-based beverages | Common | Less common |
| Clear beverage systems | Less common | Common |
| Plant tissue culture | Less common | Common |

## Choosing Between HA and LA

The choice should be based on the desired functionality rather than simply the product name.

For a plant-based beverage requiring suspension and a softer mouthfeel, HA gellan gum may be more suitable.

For a clear beverage or a firm gel system, LA gellan gum may be a better starting point.

Actual performance depends on concentration, salts, pH, processing conditions, and the other ingredients in the formulation.
